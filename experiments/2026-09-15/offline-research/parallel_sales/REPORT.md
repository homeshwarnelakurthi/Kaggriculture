# Sales timing investigation

The submitted V43 is frozen. These candidates are offline experiments and have not been submitted.

## Exact change

V43 advances an eligible scheduled sale by up to six turns during steps 288 through 695. `h7.py` increases the bound to seven; `h8.py` to eight. A byte-independent text comparison confirmed each changes only that constant. This retains all original safeguards: sell only projected available stock; exclude wheat and fertilizer; stop at pickup or product-purchase barriers; stay within the current 72-turn route block; suppress the corresponding future sale quantities through recorded debts. The existing sale-order assignment optimizer is unchanged.

Selling earlier can capture a higher price before the rival adds supply. It can also lose money when demand would have raised the price before the original sale, so mirror wins alone do not establish improvement against the wider field.

## Completed results

Each variant won 24/24 holdout games against V43 (12 seeds, both seats), with no invalid games or error telemetry. H7 averaged +1869.6 coins (minimum +417); H8 +1790.4 (minimum +385). Their maximum observed turn times were438.029ms and455.976ms.

H8 and the V43 control each won12/12 external games. H8's margin versus V42 was10,31,152 coins lower on seeds331,332,333 respectively in each seat. Against Barnyard, paired margin changes ranged from-21 to+427. All these external pairs shared the same final shop sequence.

Recommendation: preserve both variants as unsubmitted research candidates. H7 had the larger holdout margin but has not had the external regression suite; H8 has broader checks but does not dominate V43. No universal replacement was established. The next design should condition sale timing on opponent evidence, and address measured storage waste, rather than simply increasing the timing bound.
